Was the revelation permitting Muhammad's marriage to Zaynab genuine, or self-serving?

Surah 33:37, cf. 33:4-5
“

After Zayd — Muhammad's freed slave and adopted son — divorces his wife Zaynab, a verse arrives stating that God married her to Muhammad specifically so that believers would have no difficulty marrying the divorced wives of their adopted sons, and that Muhammad had privately concealed what God intended to reveal.

Context This is not a contradiction between 33:4-5 and 33:37 — the two are consistent with each other: 33:4-5 establishes that adopted sons aren't legally equivalent to biological sons, and 33:37 explicitly presents itself as the real-world precedent demonstrating that same rule, not an unrelated event. Even critical sources acknowledge this directly.

The actual dispute is over authenticity and timing, not logic: citing historian al-Tabari's account, critics argue Muhammad had grown attracted to Zaynab before Zayd's divorce, and that a conveniently-timed revelation supplied religious sanction for a marriage he already wanted — making this a question about whether the revelation was genuine or a self-interested rationalization.

Traditional and modern apologetic responses (e.g. Ibn Kathir, and contemporary writers like Kaleef Karim) reject the Tabari account as a later, unreliable fabrication intended to defame Muhammad, and read the marriage as deliberate divine legislation — using the Prophet's own life, deliberately, to break a deeply entrenched pre-Islamic taboo that could not have been dismantled by mere instruction alone.
