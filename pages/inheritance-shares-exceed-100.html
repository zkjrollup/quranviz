Do the Quran's inheritance shares add up to more than 100%?

Surah 4:11-12, 4:176
“

Consider a woman who dies. She leaves behind a husband, her mother, and two sisters - but no children. The Quran assigns each of them a fixed share: the husband gets half the estate, the mother gets a sixth, and the two sisters split two-thirds between them. Added together, those shares alone already total 4/3 of the estates. That's 33% more than the whole thing, before anyone else is even accounted for.

Context The Quran's inheritance rules are detailed, and classical jurists took this specific problem seriously rather than ignoring it. The 'awl' solution; proportionally scaling down every heir's share so the total equals the full estate - is the traditional fix, credited to Caliph Umar. But it's worth being precise about what that fix is: a correction applied on top of the text, not a resolution found within it. That sits in tension with verses describing the religion as perfected and complete (5:3) and the Book as a clarification 'for all things' (16:89) — if a specific, numerically stated rule doesn't work as written and needs an external adjustment to function, that's a harder case for those claims than an ambiguous or open-ended verse would be. Defenders respond that scholarly elaboration (ijtihad) was always understood as part of how revelation gets applied, not evidence against its completeness.
