Contradictions

Why is Adam's descent from paradise narrated more than once?

Surah 2:36, 2:38, 7:24, 20:123
“

The command for Adam (and Eve, and Iblis) to "descend" from paradise appears across several passages, told with varying detail each time.

Context Read by most commentators as narrative repetition for emphasis, a common feature of Quranic storytelling — critics point out the differing details as a sign of separate, imperfectly harmonized tellings.
Contested Wording (Arabic & Transliteration)
Surah 2:36, 7:24
اهْبِطُوا بَعْضُكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّ
ihbiṭū ba'ḍukum li-ba'ḍin 'aduww
"get down, some of you enemies to others" — plural imperative
Surah 2:38
اهْبِطُوا مِنْهَا جَمِيعًا
ihbiṭū minhā jamī'an
"get down from it, all of you" — plural again, repeating the command
Surah 20:123
اهْبِطَا مِنْهَا جَمِيعًا
ihbiṭā minhā jamī'an
"get down from it, both of you" — dual form, addressing only two (commonly read as Adam and Iblis), a grammatical difference from the plural used in Surah 2
