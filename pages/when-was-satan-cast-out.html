Contradictions

Was Satan cast out before or after tempting Adam?

Surah 15:34-35, 7:19-20 vs 2:36
“

Before the garden narrative begins, Iblis is told to leave and is cursed the moment he refuses to bow to Adam. Yet Adam is then placed in the Garden and successfully tempted by that same, already-expelled Iblis. A later verse describes Iblis, Adam and Eve all being sent down together as a single joint event, after the temptation.

Context This isn't a modern objection — classical commentators grapple with it directly. Ibn Kathir's tafsir on 2:36 asks outright how an already-banished Iblis could still access the Garden, and cites several competing explanations from earlier scholars: that he snuck in disguised inside a serpent (a detail borrowed from the Torah, not stated in the Quran itself), that he tempted Adam 'on his way out,' or that he tempted him from a distance without needing to physically enter. None of these appear in the Quran's own text. These competing explanations don't agree with each other, which suggests the sequence genuinely isn't settled by the verses alone. A minority reading, offered by the Bayan-ul-Quran commentary, argues 'get out of it' refers to Iblis abandoning a prideful state rather than leaving a physical place — but this requires reading a verb of physical exit ('ikhruj') and a term for physical banishment ('rajim,' one who is stoned/driven away) in a non-literal sense the immediate text doesn't clearly signal on its own.
