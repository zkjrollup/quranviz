Misogyny, Violence & Discrimination Against Women

Why does a woman's testimony count as half in some verses?

Surah 2:282
“

In matters of debt, calls for two male witnesses, or one man and two women, "so that if one errs, the other can remind her."

Context Classical explanation: The dominant classical tafsir (e.g., Ibn Kathir) attributes this directly to a "shortcoming in her mind" (naqṣ fī 'aqlihā) — framing it as a claim about women's comparative reliability in recollection generally, not limited to commercial contexts.

Reformist/contextual explanation: Modern scholars (e.g., Taha Jaber Al-Alwani) reject that reading, arguing the verse instead reflects that business and commercial transactions were overwhelmingly a male domain by social custom in 7th-century Arabia — meaning women in general simply had less everyday exposure to contracts and financial dealings, not that they were less capable. On this reading, a woman experienced in business (a merchant, for instance) would not be subject to the same discount.

The tension: These two explanations disagree with each other, not just with critics — one is about innate ability, the other about social exclusion from a specific domain. Historically, women's broad exclusion from formal commercial and legal training in this period is well documented; whether the verse itself intends that as its reasoning, versus a claim about cognitive difference, remains genuinely disputed among Muslim scholars themselves.
